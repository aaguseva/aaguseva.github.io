Magnetic fields play a fundamental role in the life and evolution of stars, driving turbulence and mixing throughout their interiors. They govern key stellar properties that we observe yet still struggle to fully explain, including rotation rates, magnetic activity, and chemical composition. A central challenge in stellar physics is building theoretical models that capture how these complex internal fluid motions interact. Because full-scale simulations of stellar interiors produce massive datasets and cannot yet reach realistic astrophysical conditions, this project takes a new approach: distilling vast simulation data down to its most essential physical patterns and extrapolating them to real stellar observations. By developing simplified, large-scale models of how stars generate their magnetic fields, this work will sharpen our understanding of stellar activity and internal transport - insights that will directly support the characterization of exoplanets for the upcoming PLATO mission.

This work is part of the R&D&I project EUR2026-179108, funded by MICIU/AEI/10.13039/501100011033.
